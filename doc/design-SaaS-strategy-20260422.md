## Problem Statement

Research and standards analysts at Korean ICT/telecom organizations spend 4-8 hours per week manually searching news, reading articles, and copy-pasting content into weekly/monthly trend reports. Their job credibility depends on not missing major ICT developments. When they do miss something — a new 3GPP standard, a spectrum policy shift, a key vendor move — their reports are wrong and their standing with leadership takes a hit.

The IRONAGE AI Analytics System v4.2 automates this pipeline: news collection (Google Alerts RSS, Naver News API), multi-AI analysis (GPT-4, Claude, Gemini, Perplexity), structured report generation (Google Docs), and HTML email delivery. One signed, paying customer already exists.

## Demand Evidence

- Signed contract with a paying customer (not TTA — a separate organization)
- TTA (Korean Telecommunications Technology Association) served as the internal pilot and use case inspiration
- Target user: Research/Standards analyst whose professional credibility rides on comprehensive trend coverage
- Status quo cost: 4-8 hours/week of manual work; the real cost is the anxiety of "did I miss something important?"

## Status Quo

Manual workflow: analyst manually searches Google/Naver, reads articles, selects relevant ones, writes summaries, formats them into a report template, sends by email or uploads to a shared drive. This is done weekly. The process is inconsistent, time-consuming, and produces different quality depending on who did it that week.

## Target User & Narrowest Wedge

**The Person:** A Research/Standards analyst at a Korean ICT standards body, telecom carrier research division, or government-affiliated ICT research institute. They produce weekly or monthly intelligence briefs. They are not technical. They care about coverage completeness above all else.

**The Wedge:** One customer is already signed. The second customer should be the most similar org to the first — same user type, same report cadence, same Korean-language source needs. Do not generalize the wedge yet.

**Core Value Proposition (from second opinion analysis):** This is not a "save 6 hours a week" tool. It is *insurance against professional embarrassment*. The pitch is: "You will not miss a major ICT development. Ever." That's what the analyst is actually buying.

## Constraints

- Codebase is a working prototype, not production infrastructure (SQLite, no version control, hardcoded assumptions)
- Solo founder (or small team) — no capacity for complex multi-tenant engineering right now
- Korean language and Korean news sources are a core requirement — Western tools don't serve this niche
- Institutional sales cycles: government-adjacent orgs move slowly, relationship-driven
- PIPA (Personal Information Protection Act) compliance is a potential requirement for government-adjacent Korean org customers — routing news content through US-based AI APIs (OpenAI, Anthropic) may require data processing agreements or consent provisions

## Premises

1. **Vertical depth is the moat.** Korean ICT/telecom standards workflow specificity (sources, terminology, report formats, committee structures) creates switching costs that general tools (AlphaSense, Meltwater) can't match.
2. **Replication is the path.** The signed contract is proof the model works. Find 2-3 orgs that look exactly like customer one: similar size, similar analyst role, similar report cadence.
3. **Operationalization AND sales are both bottlenecks.** *(Original: "The product is demo-ready — the bottleneck is sales, not engineering." Revised after cross-model challenge.)* The current code has one customer's assumptions baked in (hardcoded keywords, report formats, delivery targets). Trigger for Approach A (parameterization): when the manual per-customer setup time exceeds 4 hours OR when the third customer onboards.

## Approaches Considered

### Approach A: Config-First Sprint
Add a customer config YAML/JSON system that parameterizes keywords, sources, report templates, and delivery targets. One config = one customer. Deploy manually per customer. Ship in ~1 week.
- **Effort:** S | **Risk:** Low
- Fastest path to second customer with a cleaner handoff
- Still requires manual deployment per customer; doesn't scale past ~5-6 customers

### Approach B: Managed Analyst Service (White-Glove)
Don't build self-service yet. Configure and run the system for each customer yourself. They receive the report. You charge a monthly retainer. Sell the outcome ("weekly Korean ICT intelligence brief"), not the tool.
- **Effort:** S (sales, not engineering) | **Risk:** Low-Med
- Highest learning rate per customer; you discover exactly what differs before automating
- You are the ops bottleneck; doesn't scale past ~8-10 customers without hiring

### Approach C: Multi-Tenant SaaS Platform
Rebuild with proper cloud infrastructure: multi-tenant database, web-based onboarding, subscription billing, customer dashboard, CI/CD pipeline.
- **Effort:** XL (~3-4 months) | **Risk:** High
- Correct long-term architecture; VC-fundable
- Massively over-engineered for one paying customer

## Recommended Approach

**B then A.**

Run as a managed analyst service for customers 2-4. Configure the system for each customer manually. Charge a monthly retainer. Use each customer to learn exactly what differs in their setup (keywords, sources, report structure, delivery preferences, committee names, jargon). After customer 3-4, build the config system (Approach A) to stop doing it manually.

**Why B before A:** You don't yet know what to parameterize. Building a config system before you've run the system for a second customer means you'll parameterize the wrong things. Do it manually twice. Then you'll know exactly what the YAML file needs to contain.

**Sales pitch for Approach B:** "I run an AI analyst service that ensures your team never misses a major Korean ICT development. Weekly brief, your inbox, Korean-language sources, your format. ₩X/month."

**What "managed service" means operationally (weekly ops checklist per customer):**
- **Automated** (runs without founder action): news collection pipeline (Google Alerts RSS, Naver News API) — scheduled cron or manual trigger, produces raw article list
- **Manual** (founder action required): all steps below
1. Monday: trigger news collection run if not automated; review output log
2. Check article count. If <20: run supplemental Naver search on top 3 keywords manually. If still <20: note in ops log; do not delay report — low-coverage weeks still ship.
3. Trigger AI analysis pipeline; skim summary for factual errors, missed major vendor/regulatory news, or hallucinated citations
4. Push finalized report to customer's Google Drive folder
5. Confirm email delivery: check sent folder. If bounced → contact customer via phone or KakaoTalk within 2 hours
6. Log: delivery timestamp, article count, any anomalies — one row per customer per week in a spreadsheet

**Failure modes and responses:**
- AI pipeline fails → delay report by 24h maximum; notify customer proactively
- Google Drive upload fails → attach report directly to email as PDF
- Article count <10 for 2 consecutive weeks → audit source configuration (keywords may need updating)
- Customer reports factual error in report → correct, re-send, document in ops log; consider adding that topic to manual review checklist

Estimated time: 30-60 minutes/customer/week under normal conditions. 2-hour ceiling (includes exception handling). Above 2 hours/customer/week consistently = trigger for Approach A engineering work.

**Customer 2 qualification checklist (what "similar org" means):**
- Primary job function: research, standards tracking, or policy analysis in ICT/telecom
- Produces weekly or monthly intelligence reports (has an existing report template)
- Has a dedicated analyst role (not a manager who delegates one-off searches)
- Organization budget: has procurement authority for recurring services (not one-time project budget)
- Korean-language sources: ≥50% of their relevant news is in Korean

**Disqualify if:**
- Primary focus is commercial media monitoring or brand/PR (different use case, different sources)
- Non-ICT sector (finance, healthcare, etc.) — Korean sources and keyword taxonomy wouldn't transfer
- No existing report cadence (ad hoc intelligence needs → different product model)
- Budget requires full government procurement process >6 months (too slow for initial traction)

**Intake process for new managed service customers:**
1. Discovery call (30 min). Questions to ask:
   - "What do you currently read every week to track ICT trends?" → identifies sources
   - "Can you share a recent report you produced?" → reveals their format; if they can't share, ask them to describe the sections
   - "Which topics absolutely cannot be missed?" → gives top-priority keywords
   - "Who receives the report — just you, or a team?" → determines delivery list and format expectations
   - Collect: 10-20 primary keywords, delivery email(s), preferred cadence (weekly/biweekly/monthly), report language preference (Korean/English/bilingual)
2. Within 3 business days: deliver a sample one-week trial report in their format, at no charge. This is the close.
3. If they proceed: configure the system manually using the gathered inputs. No config system exists yet — update the codebase variables directly for this customer. Run one full test cycle. Confirm report receipt before the first billing date.

**Note:** "Set up config" currently means manually editing hardcoded variables in the Python scripts for each customer. This is Approach B's intentional trade-off — it is a known limitation, not an oversight. Approach A (config file system) is triggered after customer 3.

**Pricing framework (until customer 1 contract value is known):**
- Floor: ₩500,000/month per customer (justified by 4-8 hours of analyst time saved at standard Korean research org rates)
- Target: ₩1,000,000-1,500,000/month (positions as a fractional AI analyst, not a software subscription)
- If customer asks for annual contract: offer 10% discount for 12-month prepay
